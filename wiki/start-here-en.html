In 90 seconds #

Two people want to build something together: a relationship, a family, a project, or a business. What should they talk through before making an important decision?

Before We Build offers a conversation map as part of its research approach. The map helps people ask questions and discuss commitments and boundaries, plans for the future, shared work, and how they understand each other. It does not diagnose people or decide whether they are compatible.

Who it is for #

You can use the map with a partner, friend, relative, colleague, or cofounder. Start with a specific task: what do you want to do together, and what outcome do you need?

Consent must be freely given. Coercion, violence, and exploitation cannot be explained away as “different styles.” Safety matters at every stage.

Suppose you are considering a move #

Both people want to move to another city. But one worries about who will help an elderly relative, while the other is thinking about opportunities five years from now. They also need to decide who will look for housing and which information about the new city they can trust.

It helps to take the questions one at a time. What are your commitments? What do you want for the future? How will you divide the work? How does each of you make sense of what you've learned about the city? Money, health, the needs of dependants, unequal power, and safety may change any decision.

Start with what happens #

Notice who asks questions, how people divide tasks, and where they misunderstand each other. You can also see how they respond to pressure and try to repair the relationship after an argument. The internal process that might explain the behaviour cannot be observed directly.

Then consider possible explanations #

The project explores how three typologies might help frame research questions. Temporistics suggests questions about time and direction, Psychosophy about how people organize joint action, and Socionics about how they work with information. Values and moral obligations are considered separately.

Here “type” means a hypothesis about a recurring way of perceiving and organizing experience. It is not a personality type: it concerns how someone selects, orders, and interprets experience, beyond sensory perception alone. Read more about how the project reinterprets typologies. These explanations need to be tested and revised.

What else might explain it? #

A disagreement may involve values, limited money or information, unequal power, a crisis, or a habit. Experience, culture, skills, roles, stress, and health also matter. Check these explanations before turning to typology.

What the map cannot tell you #

A type does not determine moral character or dignity. It cannot establish whether a relationship is safe, whether people are ready for marriage, or whether a team will succeed. It does not predict destiny. Before We Build currently provides no numerical compatibility score.

Start the conversation #

  • What exactly do we want to build together?
  • Which commitments are non-negotiable?
  • What have we actually observed, and what are we still assuming?
  • What might change our explanation of the disagreement?
  • Can each person freely consent and speak openly?

If you want to test the approach #

Start with the validation programme to see what the project claims and how those claims could be tested. Then read about the limits of inference and possible compatibility measurement. A claim's status matters more than a type label.
