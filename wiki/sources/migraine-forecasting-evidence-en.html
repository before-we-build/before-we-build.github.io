Migraine, forecasting, and first Future — evidence review

English · Русский · Українська

Question and scope #

On 2026-10-01, a user reported an apparent association between migraine and strong forecasting abilities, described as “forecaster” and first Future in Temporistics. Numbers of cases, verified diagnoses, comparison cases, prospective forecasts, and independent position assessments were not supplied. This is a hypothesis-generating observation. The bounded search recorded in the source register found no direct test of migraine against objective forecasting or independently assessed 1F; it was not a systematic review.

What the authors claim #

Radut's preserved Captain description attributes sensitivity to changes, goal setting, routes toward goals, and attention to progress to first Future. It also allows imagined or unrealistic goals. “Forecaster” is the user's descriptor, not an established alias in this source. 1F specifies one aspect-position combination, not a full type.

Sedley proposes a predictive-processing account of migraine. Interoception studies concern perception and evaluation of bodily signals; diary studies concern anticipation of one's own headache. These authors did not validate a Temporistics position or superior prediction of external events.

Evidence supplied #

Source Design and finding What it addresses
Sedley, 2024 Theoretical account; no forecasting comparison. Proposed sensory prediction mechanisms.
Koreki et al., 2025 26 migraine cases versus 23 controls; lower heartbeat tracking, other accuracy tasks null. Task-dependent interoception discrepancies.
Jones et al., online 2025; volume 2026 58 women; no heartbeat accuracy difference between control, low-frequency and high-frequency migraine groups. A null comparison, limiting generalization.
Giffin et al., 2003 Selected diary sample: 97 usable participants; 72% of warning entries correctly predicted headache. Recognition of attack warnings; not sensitivity across all attacks.
Gago-Veiga et al., 2018 34 analyzed participants, 229 attacks; warning recorded before pain in 63 attacks; positive predictive value 85.1%. Limited coverage of attacks despite useful warnings.
ICHD-3 Clinical classification; forecasting ability is not a diagnostic criterion. Definition of migraine outcomes for research.

Limitations and rival explanations #

Samples, tasks, attack phases, and selection criteria differ. Koreki's questionnaire–performance discrepancy is not a measured neural prediction-error signal; subgroup significance did not survive false-discovery correction. Jones found an omnibus confidence difference, but no corrected pairwise significance. These findings do not establish a universal interoceptive deficit, enhancement, or causal mechanism.

Relevant alternatives include age, sex, sleep, stress, anxiety, medication, attack burden, occupation, forecasting practice, symptom awareness, retrospective reinterpretation, and selection of memorable examples. Knowing migraine status while assigning 1F can create the association being tested. Warning prediction also requires its own denominator: successful warnings and the proportion of all attacks warned about are different quantities.

What BWB accepts #

Keep four constructs separate: objective accuracy of real-world forecasts; future orientation or a proposed 1F pattern; sensory/interoceptive predictive processing; and prediction of one's own attack. They require different measurements. For example, accurately anticipating one's headache from familiar fatigue does not demonstrate accurate forecasting of a project's outcome.

BWB preserves the user's observation for testing and the papers as bounded evidence about their actual tasks. Temporistics remains a proposed model of latent processes of abduction, induction, and deduction in temporal/existential experience; a position is not an ability score.

What remains untested #

  • H1: migraine status is associated with objectively scored prospective forecasting accuracy.
  • H2: migraine status is associated with independently assessed 1F candidates.
  • H3: independently assessed 1F candidates differ in prospective forecasting accuracy.

These are separate research hypotheses. Evidence for one would not establish the others, causality, or a shared biological basis. A sufficiently precise null result or a reversed association would challenge the corresponding directional proposal.

What BWB rejects #

Reject inference that migraine identifies 1F, that 1F predicts migraine, or that migraine proves forecasting talent. Do not derive diagnosis, treatment, type verdicts, compatibility judgments, or occupational recommendations from this observation. Preserve the original assertion as a dated report, not as a settled disease–type regularity.

Researcher route and next reading #

See the proposed repository protocol at research/instruments/migraine-forecasting-hypothesis-2026-10-01.md (outside the generated wiki site). Clinical neurology should define migraine ascertainment; Temporistics research should define aspect and position evidence; psychometrics and statistics should review independent coding, blinded assessment, forecasting tasks, confounding, and preregistration before recruitment.

Useful questions: Which predictions were timestamped before the outcome? How many errors and counterexamples were counted? Were headache status and 1F assessed independently? Next: Captain (Kapitan) and Epistemic Status and Inference Limits.
